01 / Overview

Retrieval at the
event level.

A controlled setting for connecting natural language with short-term visual dynamics.

Language-based anomaly clip retrieval ranks pre-segmented short clips by their relevance to a description of a normal or anomalous event. Each CAViR clip contains one dominant event, enabling fine-grained queries such as “multiple intruders breaking into a house at night.”

Real-world anomaly collection is difficult to scale because of rare events, annotation costs and privacy constraints. CAViR (Cross-modal Anomaly Video Retrieval) provides 41,315 synthetic clips across 68 anomalous and 30 normal event types, paired with five complementary descriptions each.

We introduce FLAME to study learning from these clips and transferring to real-world retrieval. It combines token-aware expert routing with auxiliary optical-flow prediction to align event descriptions with visual appearance and motion.

Evaluation covers both text-to-video and video-to-text ranking. Temporal boundary prediction within long videos is outside this clip-level setting. Potential applications include incident investigation and workplace safety review.

01

Collect & expand

Descriptions from UCA, UCFCrime-AR and OOPS! are complemented by LLM-generated descriptions for 23 additional anomaly categories, with 50 retained descriptions per added category.

02

Generate short clips

Wan2.1 synthesizes one clip per base description. FP8 quantization and TeaCache acceleration improve generation efficiency.

03

Diversify captions

Qwen3-VL provides four additional descriptions with complementary styles and visual focus, yielding five captions for every clip.

04

Consolidate categories

LLM-assisted labeling, K-means clustering and manual verification produce 30 normal activity and 68 anomalous event categories.

Training / validation / testCategory-wise random split · approximately 7:1:2

The collection contains 16,145 normal and 25,170 anomalous clips, an approximate normal-to-anomalous ratio of 2:3. Anomalous events span biomechanical deviations, contextual inconsistencies, social norm violations and environmental anomalies.

04 / The method

FLAME

Flow-guided Language-video Alignment
with Mixture-of-Experts.

FLAME associates fine-grained event descriptions with visual appearance and short-term dynamics through two complementary modules, optimized jointly with video-text alignment objectives.

01 / Spatial representation

Token-Aware
Mixture-of-Experts

TAM adaptively routes visual and textual tokens to specialized FFN experts within the Swin-B vision and BERT text encoders. This input-dependent routing captures the varying spatial granularity of event evidence.

3 expertsTop-1 routingVision + text
02 / Motion supervision

Cross-modal
Flow Predictor

CFP fuses text with sampled-frame features using cross-attention and predicts optical flow. An MSE loss against MEMFOF pseudo-ground-truth flow supplies auxiliary supervision for short-term visual dynamics.

Text queriesFrame keys & valuesAuxiliary flow loss
Joint learning

Video-text contrastive learning · Video-text matching · Masked language modeling
Optical-flow prediction with loss weight β = 0.2

Direct synthetic-to-real transfer

Training on CAViR improves R@1 and R@10 for all three architectures on all three real-world targets, without further target-dataset fine-tuning. FLAME's T2V R@1 rises from 22.4% to 33.8% on UCFCrime-AR, from 16.5% to 20.9% on XD-Violence, and from 12.9% to 18.3% on OOPS!.
